The Four Resources Behind the Cost

Each resource adds to the bill in a different way, and each one affects how good the final model is.

Data

The text the model learns from: web pages, books, code, and licensed content, plus human-written examples for fine-tuning.
15T+ tokens
Llama 3.1 pre-training data, up from 300B tokens for GPT-3 in 2020
  • Costs: licensing deals, storage, cleaning pipelines, and paid human labelers
  • DeepSeek-V3: 14.8T tokens
Effect on performanceMore and cleaner data means broader knowledge. Poor data adds bias and errors that no amount of compute fixes.

Compute

The GPUs or TPUs that run the math, plus the data centers, networking, and cooling around them.
39.3M
H100 GPU-hours used to train the Llama 3.1 model family
  • GPT-4: about 25,000 GPUs (leaked estimate)
  • DeepSeek-V3: 2,048 H800 GPUs
Effect on performanceThe biggest cost driver. More compute allows bigger models trained on more data, which generally raises capability.

Energy

The electricity to power and cool thousands of chips running nonstop, and the carbon emissions that come with it.
1,287 MWh
to train GPT-3, producing about 552 t CO₂e, roughly 120 cars driven for a year
  • Llama 3.1 family: 11,390 t CO₂e (location-based)
  • Hardware, grid mix, and location can change emissions up to 1,000×
Effect on performanceEnergy does not improve the model directly. It scales with compute, so it is the hidden price of every capability gain.

Time

Weeks to months of continuous training, plus the months of data prep, testing, and safety work around it.
~3 months
estimated GPT-4 training run (leaked estimate)
  • DeepSeek-V3: under 2 months on 2,048 GPUs
  • One trillion tokens = about 3.7 days on that cluster
Effect on performanceLonger runs let the model see more data. But every extra week ties up expensive hardware and delays release.

Key Takeaways

95.6%

Pre-training dominates

Share of DeepSeek-V3's GPU-hours spent on one step: learning to predict the next token.

200,000×

Costs are rising fast

Frontier training costs went from about $900 in 2017 to $191M in 2023.

$5.6M

Efficiency matters

Smarter architectures and lower-precision math let DeepSeek train a strong model for far less compute.

≠ total

Headline numbers undercount

Reported figures usually cover one final run, not research, failed runs, staff, or hardware purchases.

?

Transparency gap

Closed labs disclose little, so open models like Llama and DeepSeek provide most of the hard data.
